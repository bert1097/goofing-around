Five tools for a smarter Sunday

Five ideas for a fantasy or betting tool, each with a small working demo. Play with the numbers, then pick the one worth building out. Every line, score and bet here is made up for the demo.

Parlay X-Ray

Shows what a parlay is really worth before you place it.

Every leg carries a small cut for the book. Stack legs and the cuts multiply. Build a ticket and see the true chance of hitting, what a fair book would pay, and how much of the payout you are giving up.

For
Anyone who loves a six-leg ticket
Needs
Just math to start. An odds feed later, to fill in real lines.
Effort
One weekend
Stretch
Same-game parlays, where legs move together and the book's pricing is hardest to check
Parlay X-RayExample lines
Treats the legs as independent. "Fair" is each line with the book's cut removed.

Floor or Ceiling

Start/sit advice that knows what your matchup needs.

Projections give one number per player. But if you are a big underdog you want the boom-or-bust guy, and if you are ahead you want the safe one. This tool compares full ranges of outcomes and asks one question: how many points do you need from this spot?

For
Fantasy managers stuck on a flex decision
Needs
Weekly projections plus each player's game-to-game spread, which past box scores can supply
Effort
A few weekends
Stretch
Read your league (Sleeper has a free public API) and work out the number you need for you

Rooting Guide

One screen that tells you what to cheer for.

By Sunday afternoon you have a fantasy matchup, a few bets and a survivor pick, and they do not always agree. The Rooting Guide puts everything you have riding on the day into one list, ranks the games by how much they matter to you, and flags the ones where your own bets are fighting each other.

For
People who play fantasy and bet, which is most people who do either
Needs
Your league, your open bets (typed in or pasted), and live scores
Effort
The biggest build here, because of the live data
Stretch
A "you are accidentally hedged" alert before kickoff, while you can still change something
Rooting GuideExample slate
Bar length is how much the game matters to you. "Split" means your own stakes want opposite things.

Vig Check

Finds the best price and shows what each book charges.

The same bet costs different amounts at different books. Type in the prices you see and the tool works out each book's cut, the fair price underneath, and where to place each side. Edit any number to see it update.

For
Bettors with accounts at more than one book
Needs
Manual entry to start. An odds feed makes it automatic.
Effort
One weekend by hand, a few with a feed
Stretch
A watchlist that pings you when a book drifts away from the rest

Receipts

A bet journal that tells skill from luck.

A few weeks of wins and losses says little about whether you bet well. A better test is the closing line: did you get a better number than the one the market settled on at kickoff? Receipts logs each bet, compares it to the close, and plots what you won against what your prices say you should have won.

For
Anyone who wants an honest read on their own betting
Needs
A log of your bets and closing lines for each game
Effort
A few weekends
Stretch
Break it down by bet type, so you learn that you are fine at totals and terrible at player props

Suggested order. Start with Parlay X-Ray: it is pure math, fits in one HTML file like the other projects in this repo, and can be finished in a weekend. Floor or Ceiling is the best fantasy idea and the next step up. Rooting Guide is the one with no real equivalent out there, but it depends on live data, so it makes sense as the third build once the first two have proven the pieces.

All numbers on this page are invented examples, not real odds or projections. None of these tools makes betting profitable; they show what it costs. If betting stops being fun, the US helpline is 1-800-GAMBLER.
